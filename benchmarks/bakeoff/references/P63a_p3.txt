In the above formulas, the alkyl and alkenyl groups are
preferably lower alkyls and lower alkenyls having up to 6 carbon atoms, but
they may have up to 18 carbons; while the cycloalkyl groups include cyclo-
pentyl and cyclohexyl and up to cyclononyl; and aralkyl includes thephenyl-
and naphthyl substituted lower alkanes wherein the phenyl or naphthyl group
may or may not be substituted by lower alkyl, chloro, bromo, hydroxyl and
other groups.
Many of the compounds embraced by the above formulas are
known as such, but so far as we are aware they have not been known to possess
unusually high antimicrobial activity and have not been made available in the
form of compositions prepared for use for the disinfection of various materials
subject to fungal and bacterial attack.
One of the outstanding advantages of the compositions of the
present invention is that while they are characterised by high potency,
they are free from toxic metals, like mercury and copper, which are injurious
to human beings and the lower animals. A highly effective antimicrobial com-
position for industrial use which was free from toxic metals, particularly
mercury, has long been sought but the non-metallic compounds and compositions
heretofore suggested have not been comparable in potency to the known organic
mercurials. The compositions of the present invention, on the other hand,
in addition to being freeof metal, have an activity of the order of that of
many mercurial compounds and even excel many mercurial and other metal com-
pounds now in use.
