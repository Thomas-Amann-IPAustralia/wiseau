As indicated above, the amines employed in preparing the condensates
can be cyclic amines in which each N of the formulas is part of a ring. As
disclosed hereinafter, the amine groups in each molecule of a condensation
product can be of different types; thus, one group can be cyclic and the
other not, or they can be different cyclic groups as well as different non-
cyclic groups. The cyclic amines include morpholine, piperidine, piperazine,
pyrrolidine, parathiazine, meta-thiazolidine and other, preferably saturated,
cyclic amines with or without an additional hetero atom.
One of the most useful applications which we have discovered for the
compounds employed in the present invention is in the preservation and dis-
infection of emulsions formed from "soluble oils". "Soluble oils" are in-
dustrial compositions used in metal-working as coolants, lubricants, flush-
ing agents, and anti-corrosives, after being mixed with and emulsified in
water. Other applications are as fluids in hydraulic systems and lubricants
in extrusion processes. Where metals contact metals, as in the case of cut-
ting tools in lathes, grinders, and drill presses, or even where non-metals
are extruded through metal dies, the use of these emulsified oils is essen-
tial.

plexity. Fats used may be lard, sperm oil, rape seed oil, etc. Both petro-
